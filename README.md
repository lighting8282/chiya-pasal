# Chiya Pasal

A small browser game for learning romanized Nepali. You run a tea stall (*chiya pasal*) in Kathmandu, and every customer talks to you in Nepali.

**Play it in your browser: https://lighting8282.github.io/chiya-pasal/**

**For children: [Habre's Nepali](https://lighting8282.github.io/chiya-pasal/kids.html)**, a picture-and-voice version with Habre the red panda. Kids tap big pictures to hear Nepali words in 18 topics (animals, wild animals, birds, reptiles and frogs, sea animals, bugs, colours, counting to 20, big numbers up to 100, family, food, body, clothes, things at home, nature, vehicles, feelings and everyday sayings), then play "find it" games for stars. Each topic has three levels: *Sajilo* (easy, 2 choices), *Thikka* (medium, 3 choices) and *Gaaro* (hard, 4 choices, counting to 20, listen-only). Each child gets their own player profile with their own stars. **Akshar** teaches Devanagari letters with a picture word for each, and kids can trace every letter with a finger. **Kathaa** has three short read-aloud stories about Habre, each with a question at the end. Under ⚙, grown-ups get a progress page for each child: stars per topic and level, stories read, letters written, and the words they keep missing. Voices use the browser's own speech: Microsoft Edge has Nepali voices, and Chrome reads the Devanagari with its Hindi voice. The ⚙ button lets a grown-up pick the voice and speed.

### Install it as an app

Chiya Pasal can be installed on a phone like an app. It gets its own icon, opens full-screen and works offline.

- **Android (Chrome):** open the link above, then tap ⋮ → *Install app* (or *Add to Home screen*).
- **iPhone / iPad (Safari):** open the link above, tap Share (the square with an arrow), then *Add to Home Screen*.
- **Windows / Mac (Chrome or Edge):** click the install icon in the address bar.

The game's **Settings** also has an install button where the browser supports one. Progress is kept separately in the app and in the browser.

## How to play

Play online at the link above, or download `index.html` and open it in any modern browser. There is no build step and nothing to install.

- **Greetings and small talk.** Pick the right reply, e.g. *"Tapai lai kasto cha?"* → *"Malai sanchai cha."*
- **Orders.** Read *"Malai dui plate momo chahiyo"*, put the right food on the tray, then press **Linus!** ("here you go").
- **Paying.** When a customer asks *"Kati bhayo?"* ("How much?"), add up the menu prices and pick the total in Nepali.
- **Stock questions.** *"Thukpa cha?"* → *Cha* (yes) or *Chaina* (no), depending on today's menu.

## Abhyas (practice)

The **Abhyas** button in the header opens eight short practice rounds with no timer: Mausam, Pariwar, Vishaya, Chad-parba, Dainik jivan, Samaya, Vakya and Lipi.

### Chad-parba (festivals)

Pick Dashain, Tihar or Teej. Your wife's family visits: greet them the festival way (*"Dashain ko shubhakamana!"*), receive tika (*"Dhog garchhu"*), get invited to play deusi, light lamps, eat sel roti or go to the Teej feast. A tip after each answer explains the custom.

### Dainik jivan (daily life)

Five everyday scenes: a phone call, bargaining at the market (*mol-molai*), a taxi ride, the doctor, and asking the way (*daayaa* right, *baayaa* left, *sidha* straight on).

### Samaya (time & dates)

Read a clock (*"tin bajyo"* = 3 o'clock, *saadhe* = half past, with the special words *dedh* for 1:30 and *adhai* for 2:30), name the days of the week, and learn the order of the Nepali months (*Baisakh*, *Jeth*, *Asar*…).

### Vakya (sentences)

See an English sentence and build it in Nepali from word tiles, which teaches Nepali's verb-last word order. One tile is a decoy.

### Lipi (Nepali script)

Learn the Devanagari vowels and consonants (see a letter and pick its sound, hear a sound and pick the letter), then read words you already know written in Devanagari.

### Mausam (weather)

Open **Mausam** at any time for a weather practice round, or reach day 6, when the weather starts changing at the stall. The window shows the sky (sun, heat, clouds, rain, snow, cold, wind or fog) and customers ask:

- *"Aaja mausam kasto cha?"* ("What's the weather like today?"): look outside and pick the matching sentence, e.g. *"Aaja paani pardai cha."*
- Yes/no questions like *"Ghaam lagyo?"* ("Is it sunny?"): answer *Ho* (yes) or *Hoina* (no).
- Reports from around Nepal like *"Mustang ma hawa chaldai cha"* ("It's windy in Mustang"): pick the matching weather picture.

On cold or wet days customers order more hot drinks. On hot days they want water.

### Pariwar (family)

Open **Pariwar** to practise talking with your wife's family. Six relatives come to visit, each labelled with who they are (for example *sasu*, your mother-in-law). You:

- greet each one by the right name: *Aama* and *Buwa* for your parents-in-law, *Dai* for her elder brother, *Bhai* and *Bahini* for her younger siblings, and *dhog* for her grandparents;
- answer everyday questions such as *"Khana khanubhayo?"* ("Have you eaten?"), *"Ali aru khanus na"* (eat a little more → *"Pugyo"*, that's enough), *"Kahaa jaane?"* ("Where are you going?") and *"Bujhnubhayo?"* → *"Bujhina. Pheri bhannus na."* ("I didn't understand. Please say it again.");
- with your wife, use the familiar *timi* forms, e.g. *"Ma pani timilai maya garchhu"* ("I love you too").

After each answer a tip explains the custom behind it, such as when to use *hajur*, *tapai* or *timi*. The intro screen has a table of what to call each relative and what they call you (*jwain*, *bhinaju*). There is no timer in this round.

### Vishaya (picture topics)

Open **Vishaya** to practise everyday words in 15 topics: animals, wild animals, birds, reptiles and frogs, sea animals, bugs, colours, body, clothes, things at home, nature, vehicles, feelings, fruit, and sayings people use today (including slang such as *khatra* and *dami*). A neighbour's child shows you a picture and asks *"Yo ke ho?"* ("What is this?") to name in Nepali, or asks *"… dekhaunus"* ("please show me …") and you pick the picture. Each topic opens with word cards you can tap to hear.

### Voices

Every line in the game can be read aloud. The game turns the romanized text into Devanagari and speaks it with a Nepali voice installed on your device, or a Hindi voice if there is no Nepali one. Microsoft Edge has Nepali voices built in. On Android, install Nepali in Google's text-to-speech settings. Tap 🔊 in a speech bubble to hear the line again, or tap a word to hear just that word. Settings lets you turn voices off, pick a voice and change the speed.

### Ways to answer

Settings has three extra ways to answer, which you can combine:

- **Type your answers.** Type the reply in romanized Nepali instead of picking from three. Spelling is forgiving (*aa*/*a*, *chh*/*ch* and so on).
- **Listening mode.** The customer's words stay hidden until you answer, so you have to understand them by ear. This needs a voice.
- **Answer by speaking.** A 🎤 button lets you say your answer out loud. It uses the browser's speech recognition (Chrome or Edge, online), which needs microphone access.

Tap any word in a speech bubble to see its meaning and Devanagari spelling. Peeking costs that customer's tip (*baksis*).

## Learning design

- New vocabulary comes in over six days: numbers, foods, counting words (*cup, gilas, plate, wota*), polite phrases, and on day 6 the weather. Each day opens with a card of the new words. Day 7 onward is review.
- Every word has a mastery level (0 to 5). Correct answers without peeking raise it, mistakes lower it, and weaker words show up more often.
- The **Shabdakosh** (dictionary) lists every unlocked word with its mastery.
- **Settings** has a Relaxed mode (no patience timer) and a Show English mode.
- Progress is saved in the browser's `localStorage`.

## Limitations

- Prices are multiples of 10 for the first three days. From day 4 some prices go up to amounts ending in 5, which teaches *pandhra* (15), *pachchis* (25), *paitis* (35) and so on. Other numbers from 11 to 99 are irregular in Nepali and aren't used at the stall.
- Romanized Nepali spelling varies between sources. The game uses common forms such as *dhanyabaad*, *chhito* and *bhetaula*.

## Project layout

`manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js` (offline cache) and `icons/` make the main game installable. Regenerate the icons with `python tools/make_icons.py`. The kids' game is a separate single file, `kids.html`. The main game lives in one file, `index.html`, which GitHub Pages serves from the `main` branch: styles, vocabulary data (`VOCAB`, `ITEMS`, `CHATS`), the step generators, and the game loop.

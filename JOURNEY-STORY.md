# Habre's Journey: the free map (draft outline)

**Status:** built for the free map. The game's text is a shorter version for young children, in `JOURNEY_PAGES` in `kids.html`: each section's part is split into three stops (start, middle, cliffhanger). This outline is the longer version. The Nepali needs checking by a Nepali speaker.

**The story in one line:** Hajuraama (Habre's grandmother) lives high in the mountains, where red pandas come from. Habre sets off to visit her before the first snow, with a present.

**How each chapter works:**
- There's one chapter for each section of the free map. Chapters are **required** stops on the trail. Each is the section's last stop, after the big check and before the present, so its cliffhanger leads straight on to the next section. The game already has the slot for it (`chapter` in `YATRA`).
- All other stories are **optional** side stories in the section they fit: today's stories, classics, rhymes and songs. They're there to enjoy, but they never block the trail.
- **The journey is written fresh.** Today's 15 stories aren't rewritten; they stay as side stories.
- Each chapter uses the section's 10 words (listed under it), so the story repeats what the child has just learned.
- Each chapter ends with a hook that leads to the next place.
- The last chapter leaves the door open to more maps. Every later map is one of Habre's adventures: a trip of its own that starts at home and stands alone, so the child can pick any open map next.
- Finishing a map sends **a postcard to Hajuraama** and brings home **a souvenir** for the shelf in Habre's living room. For the free map, the souvenir could be Hajuraama's old map, the one with all the other places on it.

**Who's in it:**
- **Habre**, the red panda.
- **Mimi**, the house cat, who comes along and stays with Habre all the way.
- **Hajuraama**, waiting at the end.
- A few friends met on the way.

## 0. 🏠 Habreko ghar (the house): the opening

Shown once, the first time a player reaches the map, and kept in the story so far. The letter arrives: "Come and see me before the snow falls." Grandma lives far away in the mountains, and Habre will meet lots of things on the way, so he needs new words. First he learns at home: he finds things in every room, and 10 stars open the garden gate. "Ready? Into the house!" The house repeats the reason in a banner until the gate opens.

## 1. 🌻 Bagaicha (the garden): *Hajuraamako chitthi* (A letter from Grandma)

The sun (**ghaam**) comes up, and a letter arrives: "Come and see me before the first snow. Love, Hajuraama." Habre wants to bring her a present. In the garden he picks a flower (**phul**) from the flower pot (**gamala**). Busy ants (**kamila**) and a bee (**mauri**) show him the best flowers, and a spider (**makura**) has spun a web across the gate. A butterfly (**putali**) flutters over the tree (**rukh**) towards the road. Mimi the cat (**biralo**) jumps out: "I'm coming too!" That night the moon (**jun**) shines on the road ahead.

*Hook:* the road leads out of the garden to the farm village.
*Side stories here:* *Habre ko bihana*, *Bagaichama*.

## 2. 🏘️ Gaaun (the farm village): *Mimi kahaa gai?* (Where did Mimi go?)

At the farm, Mimi chases a mouse (**muso**) and disappears. Habre asks everyone for help:
- the cow (**gai**) moos
- the chicken (**kukhura**) and her chick (**challa**) look in the straw
- the duck (**hans**) checks the pond
- the goat (**bakhra**) climbs up high to look
- the dog (**kukur**) sniffs and sniffs

They find Mimi asleep in a sack of potatoes (**aalu**). Habre's flower (**phul**) is a bit squashed. The farmer laughs and gives them a ride on her horse (**ghoda**) cart.

*Hook:* the cart is going to the market.
*Side stories here:* *Haraeko biralo*, *Bhok lagyo!*. Mimi getting lost echoes *Haraeko biralo*, so that one could move somewhere else to avoid a repeat.

## 3. 🛍️ Bazaar (the market): *Hajuraamako upahaar* (A present for Grandma)

The market is loud: cars (**kaar**), buses (**bas**), lorries (**trak**), motorbikes (**motorsaikal**) and bicycles (**saikal**). Habre and Mimi share a plate of momos (**momo**). Habre's squashed flower won't last the trip, so he looks for a better present. He walks past tomatoes (**golbheda**), onions (**pyaaj**) and balloons (**bellun**), and then sees it: a beautiful red kite (**changa**). "Hajuraama loved flying kites when she was little!"

*Hook:* the kite-seller says, "Take it to the temple square. Everyone is getting ready for the festival."
*Side story here:* *Bazaar ma*.

## 4. 🛕 Mandir (the temple square): *Chaadko saanjh* (Festival evening)

The square is glowing:
- oil lamps (**diyo**) and candles (**mainbatti**)
- drums (**dhol**) and flags (**dhwaja**)
- marigold garlands (**mala**) and flowers (**phul**)
- children with sparklers (**phuljhadi**), and someone squirting a water pistol (**pichkari**) by mistake

An old man helps Habre wrap the kite (**changa**) as a proper present (**upahaar**) and gives him a garland for Hajuraama.

*Hook:* the old man says, "The quickest way to the mountains is through the forest."
*Festival side stories here:* *Tihar aayo*, *Dashain aayo*, *Rangko chaad* (Holi).

## 5. 🌲 Jangal (the forest): *Kun baato?* (Which way?)

The tall trees (**rukh**) are dark. A rabbit (**kharayo**) and a deer (**harin**) are friendly, but a fox (**phyauro**) wants the shiny present. A monkey (**bandar**) throws fruit at the fox to chase it off, and a bear (**bhalu**) and a rhino (**gaida**) clear the path. A bee (**mauri**) and a spider (**makura**) are here too. At a fork in the path, an old lion (**singha**) asks: "Which way will you go?" The child chooses the path, and both ways lead to the water.

*Hook:* the path ends at a wide lake.
*Side story here:* *Kahaa jaane?*

## 6. 🏞️ Taal (the lake): *Dunga chadhau* (Into the boat)

Habre needs to cross the lake:
- A crocodile (**gohi**) offers a ride, with a toothy smile. Habre says no thank you. (This is a nod to *The Monkey and the Crocodile*.)
- A turtle (**kachhuwa**) is too slow, and a snake (**saanp**) is too wriggly.
- A frog (**bhyaguto**) shows them a little boat (**dunga**). Ducks (**hans**) paddle beside it, fish (**machha**) jump, and mosquitoes (**laamkhutte**) buzz around Mimi's ears.
- A deer (**harin**) drinks at the far side among the flowers (**phul**).

*Hook:* across the lake is a town with a zoo.
*Side story here:* *Paani paryo*.

## 7. 🦒 Chidiyakhana (the zoo): *Chidiyakhanama* (At the zoo)

The town has a zoo, and Habre and Mimi have never seen one:
- a giraffe (**jiraph**), a zebra (**jebra**) and an elephant (**hatti**)
- a tiger (**bagh**), a leopard (**chituwa**) and a lion (**singha**)
- a monkey (**bandar**) and a crocodile (**gohi**), just like the ones they met on the way

A big panda (**panda**) is surprised to meet a red panda: "We're not even cousins!" A penguin (**penguin**) waddles over, missing the cold. "My grandma lives where it snows," says Habre. "I'll tell her about you." The kind zookeeper is driving a truck of fruit up to the hills tomorrow, and offers them a lift.

*Hook:* an early-morning ride up into the hills.
*Side stories here:* *Schoolma*, *Habreko janmadin*.

## 8. 🍊 Suntala bagaan (the orange orchard): *Suntala tipau* (Let's pick oranges)

The truck stops at an orange orchard on a hillside. The farmer is picking oranges (**suntala**), and Habre and Mimi help. There are apples (**syau**), pears (**nashpati**) and grapes (**angur**) too. The zookeeper has mangoes (**aanp**), bananas (**kera**) and a pineapple (**bhuin katahar**) in the truck for the animals. A cheeky monkey (**bandar**) swings down from a tree (**rukh**) and runs off with an orange, and Mimi chases it under the trees, scattering the butterflies (**putali**). The farmer laughs and fills a basket of oranges for Hajuraama: "Oranges from the hills, the best winter present."

*Hook:* the farmer points up the path: "Keep climbing. The hills will take you to the mountains."

## 9. ⛰️ Pahad (the hills): *Ukaalo* (Uphill)

The path climbs. A parrot (**suga**) and a peacock (**mayur**) show off their colours, pigeons (**parewa**) and sparrows (**bhangera**) flutter about, and a goat (**bakhra**) climbs faster than Habre. Habre gets tired and sniffly, and a wise owl (**ullu**) takes care of him. When the sun (**ghaam**) comes out after the rain, there's a rainbow (**indreni**) and a butterfly (**putali**), like back home. An eagle (**chil**) circles overhead.

*Hook:* the owl says, "Hurry. The first snow is coming."
*Side story here:* *Daktarkahaa*.

## 10. 🏔️ Himal (the mountains): *Hajuraama!* (Grandma!)

Snow (**hiu**) begins to fall on the snowy mountain (**himal**). Clouds (**badal**) hide the path, and Habre and Mimi are lost. Then the moon (**jun**) and the stars (**tara**) come out, and the eagle (**chil**) guides them. Far below, a helicopter (**helikoptar**) blinks its lights. They see the glow of a fire (**aago**). It's Hajuraama, with a bear (**bhalu**) neighbour. Habre gives her the kite, the garland and the basket of oranges. Hajuraama cries happy tears, and everyone celebrates Lhosar together.

*The ending:* in the morning, an aeroplane (**hawaijahaj**) flies over the mountains. Hajuraama shows Habre an old map with many more places on it: "Where shall we go next?" From here the child picks the next adventure, and Habre sends his first postcard.
*Side stories here:* *Himalko yatra*, *Lhosar*.
